\section{Sky subtraction of the reduced frames}
\label{sec:cframes}

We now turn the prediction into a sky spectrum for each of the 1801 science
fibers of a reduced, flux-calibrated frame of the DRP, subtract it, and write
a sky-subtracted frame in the DRP format. A frame takes under a minute on
eight cores.

\subsection{From one prediction to every fiber}
\label{sec:perfiber}

We form the three median spectra of Section~\ref{sec:inputs} from the frame
itself, decompose them (Section~\ref{sec:decomp}), predict one vector of
science coefficients at the center of the IFU (Section~\ref{sec:ml}), and
estimate both corrections of Section~\ref{sec:postcorr} on the median
science spectrum. All fibers share these coefficients and corrections; they
differ only in their LSF, which matters, since a sky line rendered with the
nominal profile leaves a residual of about 20\% of its peak
(Section~\ref{sec:linescale}).

The sky fibers constrain the LSF best, because they contain only sky. We
decompose each Gaia-clean sky fiber (49 to 117 of the 119 in the frames
below) with its telescope's median LSF held fixed, and fit one surface per
spectrograph and channel to all of them,
\begin{equation}
  \phi(\Delta\lambda;\lambda,f)=\sum_{k,q,s}H_{kqs}\,M_k(\Delta\lambda)\,
  B_q(\lambda)\,S_s(f),
\end{equation}
with the M-splines $M_k$ of Section~\ref{sec:lsf} and four cubic B-splines
each in wavelength, $B_q$, and in the fiber position along the slit, $S_s$.
We require $H\ge0$ and $\sum_k H_{kqs}=1$, which keep the profile
nonnegative and of unit integral, and solve one quadratic program per block,
weighted by the observed inverse variance. We fit the $r$ and $z$ channels
and keep the survey LSF in $b$. No science spectrum enters the fit, so no
science signal can shape the profile used to subtract its sky. The rendered
sky is linear in the LSF coefficients, so all science fibers are rendered
by one matrix product.

The sky-subtracted frame keeps the DRP extensions, with \texttt{FLUX} the
sky-subtracted spectra and \texttt{SKY} the subtracted sky, and stores the
medians, their decompositions, the predicted coefficients, the corrections
and each fiber's LSF, from which any fiber's sky can be reconstructed. The
sky fibers are rendered with their telescope's median model; their residual
is no independent test, since they built that median and the LSF.

\subsection{The sky-arm correction over the full band}
\label{sec:fullband}

The reduced frames show a red counterpart of the solar pattern of
Section~\ref{sec:armcorr}. With the Moon up, the O$_2$ A and B bands leave
a residual of the same fractional depth in every decomposition. On a
full-Moon exposure it is $-2.4$, $-2.3$ and $-2.0\%$ of the sky continuum at
the head of the B band in the science, east and west medians, and $-9$ to
$-11\%$ at the A band, while the continuum around them is fitted to better
than 1\%; with the Moon down it is absent. We attribute it to the path of the
moonlight. The DRP divides every fiber by the transmission along the
science line of sight (Section~\ref{sec:inputs}), and the model's moonlight
has no absorption of its own, but scattered moonlight crosses more O$_2$,
along a path set mostly by the altitude of the Moon. The sky arms measure
the deficit, so we apply the correction over the whole band. On the held-out
observations, followed by the line scaling, this lowers the median $\chi^2$
from 0.139 to 0.077 at 5000--5800\,\AA, from 0.244 to 0.111 in the red and
from 0.366 to 0.125 in $z$, and on the OH-line pixels of the red from 0.493
to 0.064; 91\% of the observations improve. The fitted OH band scales move
by a median of 0.005, so the two corrections do not compete. Leaving the OH
lines out of the transfer gains nothing in the continuum and is four times
worse on the lines.

\subsection{Protecting the science lines}
\label{sec:lineprotect}

The safeguard of Section~\ref{sec:armcorr} rejects emission seen by one sky
field, not by both. Diffuse Galactic H$\alpha$, [N\,\textsc{ii}],
[S\,\textsc{ii}], [S\,\textsc{iii}], [O\,\textsc{ii}] or [O\,\textsc{iii}]
emission common to both fields, injected into both arms at the local
continuum level, reaches the science sky at 94--116\% of its flux, below
5000\,\AA{} as over the full band, and would be subtracted from the target.
We therefore treat 59 nebular lines from H12 to P8 in windows of
$\pm\max(500\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}},\,3\,{\rm FWHM})$ redward of 5800\,\AA{} and
$\pm\max(300\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}},\,2\,{\rm FWHM})$ blueward, 15\% of the
pixels. Dropping the correction there would leave the sky under the lines
uncorrected, so we keep what does not come from the lines. In the blue, where
the correction is the slowly varying solar pattern, we interpolate it across
each window between the medians of the clean pixels beside it; narrower
windows let a line at $\pm150$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ reach those pixels. In the red
the windows hold OH and [O\,\textsc{i}] airglow, which interpolation cannot
follow. There we keep the correction in proportion to the OH fraction of the
predicted sky and set it to zero in a core of $\pm(150\,{\rm
km\,s^{-1}}+1.5\,{\rm FWHM})$, where a nebular line on an OH line would
pass. For pointings in the LMC and SMC we add cores at their systemic
velocities, 262 and 146\,km\,s$^{-1}$.

Of 18 lines injected into both arms of 400 held-out observations, at rest,
at $\pm150$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ and at 262\,km\,s$^{-1}$ with the LMC cores, none
is transferred by more than 0.2\% at the 90th percentile, including
[O\,\textsc{i}]\,6300 on its airglow line and [S\,\textsc{iii}]\,9069, 9531
on OH lines. Inside the windows, on observations without nebular emission,
the median $\chi^2$ in the blue, green, red and $z$ is 0.114, 0.070, 0.204
and 0.245, against 0.240, 0.149, 0.235 and 0.309 with the correction dropped
and 0.076, 0.064, 0.095 and 0.118 without protection. We cannot center the
cores on the emission of each frame: the OH residual under H$\alpha$ in the
sky arms exceeded five times the photon noise on all 400 observations, so no
threshold detects nebular emission there. Gas beyond $\pm150$\,km\,s$^{-1}$
outside the Magellanic Clouds can therefore pass on OH lines in the red, a
quarter of its flux at the median at 260\,km\,s$^{-1}$.

\subsection{Results}
\label{sec:cframe-results}

Figure~\ref{fig:cframe} compares the sky subtraction of version 1.3.2 of the
DRP with ours for one exposure taken with the Moon up (exposure 12820; the
Moon at $30^\circ$ altitude, illumination 0.83, $62^\circ$ from the field).
The frame before subtraction shows what must be removed: sky lines in every
fiber, the O$_2$ bands, the sky continuum and the stars of the field as bright
horizontal bands. After either subtraction the stars remain, as they should,
and the nebular emission appears as vertical lines at
[O\,\textsc{ii}]\,3727 and H$\alpha$. The DRP frame also holds sky-line
residuals of both signs and a continuum that is negative across the whole
range; ours has both at much lower levels. We measure the differences on the
median science spectrum, outside the science-line windows and the sky lines,
in percent of the sky continuum of our frame, and on the sky-line pixels in
percent of the line flux. The 47 of 1801 fibers masked in every pixel of any
of the three frames are neither shown nor counted.

\paragraph{Continuum color.} The DRP over-subtracts the continuum. Its median
science spectrum lies 6 to 10\% of the sky below zero at all wavelengths
($-9.7\%$ at 3600--4300\,\AA, $-10.3\%$ at 6960--7580\,\AA{} and $-6.3\%$ at
8900--9800\,\AA), which no field can produce, since the stars and the diffuse
light of the field add light. Ours lies at $+2.5\%$ at 3600--4300\,\AA{} and
rises smoothly to $+11\%$ at 8900--9800\,\AA, as the starlight of a field
should. The two subtractions thus differ in color, not only in scale: the sky
removed by the DRP exceeds ours by 12\% of the sky at 3600--4300\,\AA, 15\% at
5000--5800\,\AA{} and 17--19\% at 6960--9800\,\AA. The sky fields lie several
degrees or more from the science field (Section~\ref{sec:problem}), and the
moonlight and the zodiacal light, which have different colors, differ in
strength among the three pointings. The transfer predicts the two components
separately, from the sky fields to the science field, through the viewing
geometry (Section~\ref{sec:ml}). Subtracting a measured sky spectrum directly
cannot, and a wrong mix of the two shows up as a wrong color.

\paragraph{Sky lines.} We sum the absolute residual of the median science
spectrum, after removing its 50\,\AA{} running median, over the sky-line pixels
and divide by the summed flux of the lines. The residual is 15.7, 9.3 and
5.8\% of the line flux in the DRP frame at 5800--7580, 7580--8900 and
8900--9800\,\AA, and 3.8, 1.4 and 1.6\% in ours. In single fibers beyond
7580\,\AA{} the median residual is 11.1 and 7.8\% against 5.1 and 4.7\%; the
photon noise of one fiber, which no model removes, is the larger part of ours.
The atomic lines agree: the DRP frame retains 21, 32 and 32\% of
[O\,\textsc{i}]\,5577, 6300 and 6364, ours 0.9, 1.3 and $-0.6\%$. We render
the lines from the fitted OH families, scaled on the science spectrum, with the
LSF of each fiber (Sections~\ref{sec:linescale} and~\ref{sec:perfiber}).

\paragraph{Noise.} The comparison so far rests on one exposure. A subtraction
can also remove the sky and still add noise, so we measure the scatter of the
residuals on a larger sample and compare it with the photon noise of the
unsubtracted frame, $\sigma_{\rm IVAR}=1/\sqrt{\rm IVAR}$ of the
\texttt{lvmCFrame}, which does not include the uncertainty of the sky model; a
ratio of 1 means that the residual has the noise of the data alone. Figure~\ref{fig:cframe-stats} uses
10 science fibers of low continuum (ranked by median flux, from the 40th
percentile up) in each of 634 exposures of 900\,s taken between MJD 60355 and
60402. The first row shows the pixel values in windows at
[O\,\textsc{ii}]\,3727, [O\,\textsc{iii}]\,5007, H$\alpha$ and
[S\,\textsc{iii}]\,9069, outside the science lines and the sky lines, and in a
window of OH lines near 8900\,\AA. Ours is narrower and more peaked than the
DRP in every window, and the DRP has more weight on the negative side. The
second row divides the negative-side $1\sigma$ of the pixel values at each
wavelength, the median minus the 16th percentile, by $\sigma_{\rm IVAR}$; we
leave out the positive side because it carries the faint continuum of the
fibers. In the four continuum windows the ratio is nearly flat in wavelength,
with medians of 1.24, 1.34, 1.43 and 1.38 for ours and 1.39, 1.82, 1.84 and
1.72 for the DRP. On the OH lines it is 1.47 against 2.11, with larger
excursions at single lines. The DRP residuals thus exceed the photon noise by
up to a factor of 2.1, ours by at most 1.5. The third and fourth rows show the
median, the central 68\% and 95\% of the pixel values of all fibers at each
wavelength, and $\pm\sigma_{\rm IVAR}$ over the full range. The median lies
within $0.8\,\sigma_{\rm IVAR}$ of zero for both subtractions, and the ranges
widen toward the blue together with $\sigma_{\rm IVAR}$. The 95\% range of the
DRP spikes at the OH lines throughout $r$ and $z$, where ours is much weaker;
both spike beyond 9300\,\AA.

The residual noise also averages down better. In 6600--6660\,\AA, after
removing the mean of each fiber in the window, the rms of the mean of $N$
fibers drawn at random from all 6340 spectra follows $N^{-1/2}$ to within
20\% up to $N=50$ in both subtractions; for one fiber it is $0.103\times10^{-14}$
in ours and $0.109\times10^{-14}$\,erg\,s$^{-1}$\,cm$^{-2}$\,\AA$^{-1}$ in the
DRP. At $N=1000$ it lies a factor of 1.5 above $N^{-1/2}$ in ours and 2.0 in
the DRP, and at $N=5000$ a factor of 2.4 and 3.9, so a component common to the
fibers survives the average, 1.7 times larger in the DRP at $N=5000$. Within
one exposure the excess appears sooner. For the 1792 usable science fibers of
exposure 47477 the rms stays within 15\% of $N^{-1/2}$ up to $N=30$ in ours
and $N=10$ in the DRP, and lies a factor of 3.8 and 6.5 above it at $N=1500$.
Stacking many fibers of one frame, as for a diffuse source, is thus limited by
the residuals that its fibers share and not by photon noise, and our
subtraction lowers that limit by about 40\%.
